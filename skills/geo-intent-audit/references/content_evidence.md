# Content-level GEO evidence base (for the geo-intent-audit rubric)

Compiled 2026-10-19. Method: fetched each primary source directly (arXiv HTML full text, Google Search Central pages, publisher study pages). Quotes are <15 words, one per source. Numbers are copied from the fetched pages; anything I could not open in full is marked UNVERIFIED.

Quality labels: A = primary (paper, official docs, original data owner). B = secondary / vendor original-data study (publisher's own page, correlational, vendor interest). C = marketing or unverified (do not build rubric items on it).

## 0. Read this first: what the evidence does and does not say

1. Google's own docs (A, May and Jul 2026) say there is nothing special to do for AI Overviews / AI Mode beyond core SEO and unique, non-commodity content, and explicitly call chunking, llms.txt, rewriting for AI, and inauthentic mentions unnecessary.
2. Academic GEO results conflict. The original GEO paper (KDD 2024) reports large visibility gains from statistics, quotations and citations, measured as word count and position share of a generated answer on a simulated engine. C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025 poster) re-tested the same transformations on citation rank and found them mostly ineffective or harmful, with classic retrieval rank dominating. The metric difference (share of answer text vs. citation order) explains much of the gap. A rubric must therefore treat "add statistics / quotes / citations" as quality-of-content checks, not as proven ranking levers.
3. Nothing found was evaluated on logistics / 3PL / local-service pages. All effect sizes are from generic QA, e-commerce, or debate query sets. Applicability to WareMatch is by analogy only.
4. Correlational vendor studies (brand mentions, YouTube mentions, freshness) are B at best. Ahrefs itself warns "correlation isn't causation".

## 1. Academic studies

### 1.1 GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (arXiv 2311.09735, KDD 2024) - A
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735 (HTML v3 read in full). Authors Aggarwal, Murahari, Rajpurohit, Kalyan, Narasimhan, Deshpande. Submitted 16 Nov 2023, v3 28 Jun 2024.
- Method: black-box "generative engine" = retrieve top-5 Google results per query, then gpt-3.5-turbo answers with citations; 5 samples at temperature 0.7. One randomly chosen source per query is rewritten with one of 9 methods by an LLM; relative change in that source's "impression" is measured. Also run on Perplexity.ai.
- Benchmark: GEO-bench, 10K queries (8K/1K/1K split) from 9 sources (MS MARCO, ORCAS-I, Natural Questions, AllSouls, LIMA, Davinci-Debate, Perplexity Discover, ELI5, GPT-4-generated); 25 domains; 80% informational, 10% transactional, 10% navigational. No logistics or B2B-services domain.
- Metrics: Position-Adjusted Word Count (words in sentences citing the source, weighted by citation position) and Subjective Impression (7 G-Eval dimensions scored by GPT-3.5: relevance, influence, uniqueness, subjective position, subjective count, click likelihood, diversity), both normalised so a response sums to 1.
- Baseline (no optimisation): 19.3 on both (Table 1).
- Results, absolute Position-Adjusted Word Count (Table 1): Quotation Addition 27.8; Statistics Addition 25.9; Fluency Optimization 25.1; Cite Sources 24.9; Technical Terms 23.1; Easy-to-Understand 22.2; Authoritative 21.8; Unique Words 20.7; Keyword Stuffing 17.8 (below baseline).
- Headline: best methods "improve upon baseline by 41% and 28%" on the two metrics; Cite Sources, Quotation Addition and Statistics Addition gave "30-40%" (Position-Adjusted Word Count) and "15-30%" (Subjective Impression). Fluency and Easy-to-Understand gave 15-30%.
- Keyword stuffing: "little to no improvement"; on Perplexity it scored 10% worse than baseline on Position-Adjusted Word Count (21.9 vs 24.1), though higher on Subjective Impression (28.1 vs 24.7).
- Authoritative / persuasive tone: "no significant improvement" overall; helps only on debate and history-type queries (Table 3).
- Rank effect (Table 2, relative visibility change by SERP rank): Cite Sources -30.3% for rank-1 sources, +115.1% for rank-5 sources; Quotation Addition -22.9% / +99.7%; Statistics Addition -20.6% / +97.9%. Gains concentrate in lower-ranked sources; top-ranked pages can lose.
- Combination: Fluency Optimization + Statistics Addition best pair, beating any single method by "more than 5.5%" (200-example subset only).
- Perplexity.ai (Table 5): Quotation Addition 22% over baseline on Position-Adjusted Word Count; Statistics Addition up to 37% on Subjective Impression; Cite Sources up to 9%.
- Domain dependence (Table 3): Cite Sources best for factual/"Statement" queries and Law & Government; Statistics best for Law & Government, Debate, Opinion; Quotation best for People & Society, Explanation, History.
- Limitations (authors): methods "may need to adapt over time as GEs evolve"; query nature changes; did not evaluate effect on search rankings; labelling noise. My additions: the simulated engine is gpt-3.5 with 5 sources (modern engines retrieve differently); metric is share of answer text, not whether a page is chosen at all; the LLM rewrites sources (so "statistics" may be plausible-looking inserted numbers, not verified facts); no human-quality check on rewrites in the main metric; the 2nd engine is one proprietary product tested once.
- Rubric implication: reward verifiable statistics with named sources, attributed quotations, and clear readable prose; do not reward keyword repetition or hype tone.

### 1.2 AutoGEO: What Generative Search Engines Like (arXiv 2510.11438) - A (preprint, CMU)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11438 (HTML v1 read, dated 13 Oct 2025). Authors Wu, Zhong, Kim, Xiong.
- Method: for each query an LLM-based engine answers; the pair of retrieved documents with the largest visibility gap is explained by an LLM (Explainer), distilled (Extractor), merged hierarchically (Merger), and filtered into preference "rules". Rules are used (a) in a prompt to rewrite documents (AutoGEO-API) and (b) as RL rewards for a small model (AutoGEO-Mini, Qwen3-1.7B). Engines: Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3 Haiku. Visibility = Word + Pos + Overall from the GEO paper. Also measures "generative engine utility" (DeepResearchGym: KPR, KPC, precision, recall, clarity, insight).
- Benchmarks: GEO-Bench (8,000 train / 1,000 test queries); Researchy-GEO (research queries, 5 ClueWeb22 docs each); E-commerce (1,667 train / 416 test queries from LMSYS-Chat-1M).
- Effect sizes: "average improvement of 35.99%" in GEO metrics while keeping utility; AutoGEO-API up to 50.99% over best baseline (Fluency Optimization); AutoGEO-Mini average 20.99%. On Gemini/Researchy-GEO, Overall visibility 20.18 vanilla to 43.76 (API) and 38.53 (Mini). Rewrites lifted the lowest-visibility documents from 9.46 to 35.83 (Overall, API).
- Baseline replication on its own setup (Gemini, Table 1): Fluency Optimization was the best hand-made method on all three datasets (e.g. 27.75 vs vanilla 20.18 on Researchy-GEO); Keyword Stuffing roughly flat or worse on GEO-Bench (18.05 vs 19.44); Authoritative small gains (22.11 vs 19.44 on GEO-Bench).
- Learned preference rules (Gemini engine, Tables 7-8; the most useful list for a rubric). Common across datasets: attribute claims to credible sources with citations; comprehensive coverage of key aspects and sub-topics; factual accuracy and verifiability; neutral, non-promotional tone; logical structure with headings, lists, tables; clear concise language with jargon defined; current information; no verbosity. Unique to research queries: conclusion first ("State the key conclusion at the beginning"), in-depth how/why, topic focus (no nav links or ads), specific evidence (data, statistics, named examples), balanced view, self-contained. Unique to e-commerce: step-by-step / actionable, production details (names, model numbers, specs, quantifiable data), modular self-contained units, term definitions, justify recommendations with pros and cons, non-exaggerated.
- Cross-engine: rule overlap Gemini-GPT 78.95%, Gemini-Claude 84.21%, GPT-Claude 84.21% (keyword-annotated Jaccard); across domains, open-domain vs e-commerce overlap only 34.78% to 40.00%. Engine-specific rules did best but transferred rules still beat baseline. Ablation: every single rule helped; the full set beat any single rule.
- Limitations: small cheap engine LLMs (Haiku-3, 4o-mini, Flash-Lite) not production AI Overviews; rules are LLM-derived (not human-validated) and the "ablation" shows additive prompt-following, not causal proof for a real search engine; the paper reports no human study; rewrites were produced by an LLM using the rules, so gains also reflect general polish; no 3PL or B2B domain; preprint (not peer reviewed at time of reading).
- Rubric implication: this is the best single evidence-based checklist of "what the engine's LLM prefers": answer-first, specific evidence, sourcing, neutral tone, structure, self-contained passages, currency.

### 1.3 C-SEO Bench: Does Conversational SEO Work? (arXiv 2506.11097, NeurIPS 2025 poster) - A (counter-evidence)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097 (HTML v3, 20 Oct 2025). Authors Puerto, Gubri, Green, Oh, Yun.
- Method: 6 domains (Retail 500 queries, Video Games 436, Books 249 for product recommendation; Web 300, News 294, Debate 142 for QA), >1.9K queries, ~16K docs. Engines: gpt-4o-mini, claude-3-5-haiku, o3, o4-mini. Tests the 8 GEO-paper transformations plus "Content Improvement" (all combined) and "LLM Guidance" (markdown summary prepended). Metric: citation rank improvement; Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm-Bonferroni; also multi-adopter game (adoption rate 0-100%).
- Findings: "most current C-SEO methods are not only largely ineffective but also frequently have a negative impact on document ranking" (abstract). Of 54 cases only 3 were significant (Content Improvement on Retail; LLM Guidance on Retail and Games); no method worked for QA or on Haiku 3.5. Statistics "decreases rankings in 19 out of 24 evaluated settings". Example mean rank gains for gpt-4o-mini: Statistics -0.53 (Web) and -0.80 (Debate); Quotes mostly about 0. Best traditional SEO (improving the doc's retrieval rank / position in context) gave +2.77 (Retail) vs +0.18 for Content Improvement. As adopters increase, gains shrink ("congested and zero-sum").
- Limitations (authors): white-hat methods only; English only; proprietary models; no interplay test between SEO and C-SEO; documents fit in context (no summarisation of long docs).
- Why it conflicts with 1.1: GEO paper measures word share of the answer, C-SEO measures citation order; the latter "does not measure the LLM preference" per authors' explanation.
- Rubric implication: do not claim a quantified lift from statistics/quotes. Content edits are secondary to being retrieved in the first place (indexed, ranking for the query or its fan-out queries). Keep content improvements justified by reader utility.

### 1.4 E-GEO: Testbed for GEO in E-Commerce (arXiv 2511.20867) - A (preprint)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20867 (HTML read; v1 25 Nov 2025; a later version dated July 2026 adds red-teaming per search summary, UNVERIFIED which version I read).
- Method: 13,747 multi-sentence consumer product queries each with 10 Amazon listings; 5 generative engines, 7 LLM rewriters, 15 hand-crafted heuristics; prompt meta-optimisation. Limitations stated: English only, North-American / durable-goods skew (r/BuyItForLife), Amazon-only listings, results conditional on all-MiniLM-L6-v2 + GPT-5-mini retrieval.
- Finding: optimised rewrite prompts converge on a shared playbook (opening summary, section headings, scannable bullets, use cases, FAQ-style answers to user questions, keywords and synonyms, no keyword stuffing, preserve factuality). Authors call it a "universally effective" strategy; I did not extract per-heuristic effect sizes (UNVERIFIED numbers).
- Rubric implication: for commercial pages, structured attribute-labelled bullets, use-cases, answered buyer questions, and factual preservation are the convergent signals. Closest academic analogue to WareMatch commercial-intent pages.

### 1.5 Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search (arXiv 2509.08919) - A/B (preprint, observational)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919 (HTML read). Authors Chen, Wang, Chen, Koudas. Submitted 10 Sep 2025. Data collected Aug 2025.
- Method: controlled query sets across verticals, languages and paraphrases (e.g. 1,000 consumer ranking prompts, 10 categories, US and Canada) comparing Google with web-enabled ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini; sources classified Brand / Earned / Social with a proprietary, partly AI-assisted scheme; overlap via top-k and Jaccard.
- Findings: "systematic and overwhelming bias towards Earned media". Examples: Automotive US, Google 45.1% Earned vs AI search 81.9% Earned and 0% Social; consumer electronics Claude 93.7% / GPT 93.6% Earned; Perplexity more mixed (31.6% Brand / 53.3% Earned / 15.1% Social). Freshness: Claude mean cited-article age about 117 days (median 62) in consumer electronics but about 331 days (median 148) in automotive with 61% date coverage.
- Limitations (authors): snapshot in time, proprietary and subjective source taxonomy, engines change quickly. Verticals are consumer ranking queries, not B2B logistics.
- Rubric implication: for "best X" comparison queries, AI engines draw mostly from third-party review/editorial sources, so a brand-owned page alone is unlikely to be the cited source; off-page third-party coverage matters. Gives a reason to check for corroboration and external references, not on-page tricks.

### 1.6 Recency bias in LLM rerankers (arXiv 2509.11353) - A (controlled experiment, not a deployed engine)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11353 (HTML read).
- Method: artificial publication dates prepended to passages in TREC DL21/DL22; 7 models (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o, GPT-4, LLaMA-3 8B/70B, Qwen-2.5 7B/72B); listwise reranking and pairwise preference tests.
- Effect: "fresh" passages promoted; Top-10 mean publication year shifted forward by up to 4.78 years; individual items moved up to 95 ranks; pairwise preference reversed by up to 25% on average. Larger models attenuate but do not eliminate it.
- Limitation: synthetic dates on a passage-retrieval benchmark, not live engines; a date shown in text is not the same as a crawl-detected date.
- Rubric implication: a visible, truthful "last updated" date on the page is cheap and plausibly helps rerankers; fake date bumps are an integrity risk (and see section 3 where Google AIO showed no freshness preference).

### 1.7 GEO-SFE structural features (arXiv 2603.29979, Mar 2026) - B/C (low confidence)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29979 (HTML read). Authors affiliated with Univ. of Tokyo / Tsukuba. Claims "consistent 17.3% citation improvements" (Citation Rate 45.0% to 52.8%, paired t-test, Cohen's d = 0.64) from restructuring content (macro: document architecture, meso: chunking, micro: visual emphasis).
- Method: 200 GEO-bench articles (~33 per domain, avg 2,547 words), 377 queries, "six engines" listed as Google SGE, Bing Chat, Perplexity, Phind, ChatGPT, Claude; 2,400 test cases; semantic similarity 0.843 after restructuring.
- Why low confidence: engine list includes retired products (SGE, Bing Chat), I found no limitations section, structure is LLM-applied, and it contradicts C-SEO Bench's weak/negative results for "Content Improvement" (which includes bolding and structure). Treat as hypothesis, not evidence. Not peer reviewed as far as I could see.
- Rubric implication: structure checks are justified by Microsoft/Google readability guidance and AutoGEO rules, not by this paper's 17.3%.

## 4. What official docs say about passage-level retrieval

- Google: passages are understood within pages ("better understand the relevancy of specific passages", 2020; "show the relevant piece to users", 2026 guide), and AI features use RAG over the Search index plus query fan-out (G3). Google explicitly says no chunk-size requirement and no ideal length (G8, G9).
- Microsoft/Bing: pages are parsed into modules that are ranked and assembled into answers (G21); headings act as "chapter titles that define clear content slices" (same page; paraphrased from the post). No sizes given.
- OpenAI/Anthropic published chunking parameters (G25, G26) are for developers' own corpora (file search, document citations) and say nothing verified about how ChatGPT search or Claude web search chunk public pages.
- Net: official sources support "clear, self-contained, well-headed sections" but give no number to check against. Any rubric item with a word-count threshold for passages would be unsupported.

## 6. Recommended rubric additions (derived only from the evidence above)

1. Answer-first: direct answer in first 1-2 sentences of the page and of each section (AutoGEO "Conclusion First"; Microsoft Q&A pairs). Evidence: A (preprint), no isolated effect size.
2. Non-commodity test (Google): at least N page-specific facts WareMatch can defend; flag generic filler. Make the check qualitative with a fact count, not a magic number.
3. Evidence quality: each statistic has a named, dated, linked source; no unsourced numbers; quotations only if real and attributed. Do not promise a lift (GEO vs C-SEO conflict).
4. Fan-out coverage: list the sub-questions implied by the head query and check each is answered or linked within the topic cluster. Pair with an anti-scaled-content check (location-page near-duplicates).
5. Self-contained, modular sections with descriptive headings; tables for comparable data; no key content only in tabs/accordions/PDFs/images.
6. Entity clarity and local facts: facility/city, address, services, certifications, measurable specs; consistent with GBP and schema.
7. Freshness: visible "last updated" date, genuine updates, dated time-sensitive facts. Weight lower for Google AIO (no freshness preference in Ahrefs data), higher for ChatGPT-type assistants.
8. Negative flags: keyword stuffing, hype/authoritative tone without substance, unsourced superlatives, thin templated location pages, hidden content, fake dates.
9. Hygiene (not scored as GEO lift): indexable, snippet-eligible, schema matches visible text.
10. Reporting rule: label every rubric item with its evidence grade (A / B / C) and state "no proven effect size for 3PL pages". Do not include word-count targets for passages or schema/llms.txt as AI lifts.
