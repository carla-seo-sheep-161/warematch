# US compliance facts for 3PL/warehouse page (primary sources only)

Researched 2026-10-19. Government/court sources only; no company or vendor sources.
Confidence: A = read at the primary source in this session. B = secondary / not read directly.
Quotes are short, from fetched text. Not legal or tax advice; state rules are fact-specific.

## 1. Sales tax: inventory/warehouse presence, Wayfair, marketplace facilitators

### 1.1 Wayfair overruled the physical-presence-only rule (A)
- Source: South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc., 585 U.S. 162 (decided June 21, 2018). Supreme Court of the United States, preliminary print of U.S. Reports.
- URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/585us1r58_pok0.pdf (slip opinion: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/17-494_j4el.pdf, returned 404 on later re-fetch; the cached copy of the preliminary print was read)
- Date on document: June 21, 2018.
- Quote: "the physical presence rule of Quill is unsound and incorrect"
- Second quote (nexus on the facts): "the nexus is clearly sufficient based on both the economic and virtual contacts"
- Takeaway: A state can require a seller to collect sales tax based on sales into the state, not only physical presence. Stored inventory is a separate, additional trigger in state law (below).
- Note: The Court decided the Commerce Clause question. State thresholds (e.g. $100,000 in South Dakota) come from state law and vary.

### 1.2 California: inventory in an in-state fulfillment center = engaged in business (A)
- Publisher: California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA).
- URL: https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/fulfillment-centers/
- Date: no last-updated date shown (accessibility certification on page dated June 2025).
- Quote: "is considered to be engaged in business in California."
- Context: applies to a seller outside California that stores inventory in a California fulfillment center. Must register, file returns and pay tax.
- Takeaway: Putting stock in a California 3PL/fulfillment site creates a California registration and collection duty, even if the seller is otherwise out of state.
- Additional CDTFA material (B, only seen in search summaries, not fetched): Local and District Tax Guide, https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/local-and-district-retailer-taxes/online-retailers-registration-and-local-tax.htm, distinguishes commingled vs dedicated stock at third-party sites. Verify before citing.

### 1.3 Texas: warehouse/distribution center is a physical location (A)
- Publisher: Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts, Publication 94-108 "Engaged in Business".
- URL: https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-108.php
- Date: shown as "94-108 (12/2020)" (publication revision); no page-updated date.
- Quote: "distribution center, sales room, sample room, warehouse, storage place"
- Takeaway: In Texas a warehouse or storage place in the state is listed among physical locations that make a seller engaged in business there.
- Related (A): https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php , no date shown. Says: "If you have a physical presence in this state." (you are not a remote seller). That page does not mention inventory or warehouses.
- Marketplace FAQ (A): https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers-marketplace-faq.php , no date shown. Quote: "tangible personal property temporarily stored in Texas at a marketplace provider's facility". Under the $500,000 safe harbor a seller with such stock needs no permit if the marketplace provider has certified it will assume seller duties. Above the threshold, a permit and collection are required (second part is B: from search summary of the same FAQ).

### 1.4 New York: stored inventory at an unaffiliated fulfillment center is fact-specific (A)
- Publisher: NY Department of Taxation and Finance, Advisory Opinion TSB-A-24(52)S, dated November 12, 2024.
- URL: https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/advisory_opinions/sales/24-52s.htm
- Quote: "does not create nexus for the seller" (when the center only does listed services such as shipping from inventory)
- Takeaway: NY has said, for one petitioner, that stock at a limited-service fulfillment provider alone did not create nexus, but this is not a general rule. The opinion binds the Department only as to that petitioner and its facts. Do not present as a safe harbor for brands.
- Contrast: California and Texas treat in-state inventory/warehouse as presence. Brands cannot assume one rule.

### 1.5 Marketplace facilitator interaction (partly A)
- NY opinion above (A): seller was not required to register because the marketplace provider collected tax on its sales through its website, so long as the seller makes no other NY sales.
- Texas FAQ above (A/B) shows facilitator certification affecting a seller's stored inventory.
- Takeaway: Marketplace facilitator laws shift collection on marketplace sales to the platform, but sales through your own site from the same inventory are still yours. Check each state.

### 1.6 Streamlined Sales Tax Governing Board and Multistate Tax Commission
- SSTGB: https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/ (A for homepage only). Homepage news says Wayfair "grants states that have implemented certain simplifications the authority to enforce their sales tax laws on remote sellers." No statement on inventory/warehouse nexus or economic nexus found.
- MTC: https://www.mtc.gov/uniformity/ (A for page): navigation only, no nexus, inventory or Wayfair text. Not usable as a source for these claims.
- NOT VERIFIED at SSTGB/MTC: any statement on inventory creating nexus. Use state DOR pages instead.

## 2. Foreign-Trade Zones and CBP bonded warehouses

### 2.1 FTZ definition and duty benefits (A)
- Publisher: International Trade Administration (U.S. Department of Commerce), FTZ Board.
- URL: https://www.trade.gov/about-ftzs
- Date: none shown on page.
- Quote: "designated sites licensed by the Foreign-Trade Zones (FTZ) Board"
- Quote (benefits): "Duty Deferral. Customs duties and federal excise tax deferred on imports."
- Also on page: "Duty Exemption. No duties on or quota charges on re-exports."
- Takeaway: Imported goods can sit in a zone without paying duty until they enter U.S. commerce; re-exports pay none.
- Who can use: page does not state a plain eligibility rule. Users operate in a zone under an agreement with a zone operator; production activity needs FTZ Board authorization. Regulations (A): 15 CFR 400.11 says the board may approve zones "in or adjacent to ports of entry" (adjacency = within 60 miles or 90 minutes of a port boundary). Source: 15 CFR 400.11, Foreign-Trade Zones Board, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-IV/part-400 (eCFR current as of 2026-10-15).
- FTZ pages also say retail trade is prohibited (B, search summary of trade.gov; not read verbatim).
- A trade.gov landing page (https://trade.gov/ftz) gave no definitions; points to FTZ Act, 19 U.S.C. 81a-81u, and 15 CFR Part 400.

### 2.2 CBP bonded warehouse (A)
- Publisher: U.S. Customs and Border Protection.
- URL: https://www.cbp.gov/document/publications/us-customs-and-border-protection-bonded-warehouses
- Date shown: "Last Modified: Aug 12, 2026" (attached PDF dated 11/12/2013).
- Quote: "building or other secured area in which imported dutiable merchandise may be stored"
- Quote (time limit): "up to 5 years from the date of importation"
- Authority cited: 19 U.S.C. 1555; operation in 19 CFR Part 19.
- Classes (A): 19 CFR 19.1 (eCFR, current as of 2026-10-15) lists classes; Class 2 is "Importers' private bonded warehouses", Class 3 is "Public bonded warehouses used exclusively for the storage of imported merchandise."
- Takeaway: Duty is deferred, not waived, while goods are in a bonded warehouse (duty-deferral point per CBP page; re-export/withdrawal details B from search summary). Only a bonded facility can do this, so ask a 3PL whether it is bonded.
- Who may operate: CBP page does not say; regulations govern via port director approval (B).
- Not verified: whether a given 3PL serves e-commerce DTC under these programs; check each facility.

## 3. Hazardous materials (DOT/PHMSA, 49 CFR)

Note: phmsa.dot.gov pages returned HTTP 403 to fetches here. Facts below read at 49 CFR via eCFR (A, current as of 2026-10-15). PHMSA lithium page claims remain B (search summary only): https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries.

### 3.1 Who the HMR covers (A)
- Publisher: U.S. DOT (PHMSA), 49 CFR 171.1, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-171/section-171.1
- Quote: "each person who offers a hazardous material for transportation in commerce"
- Also: the HMR are "49 CFR parts 171 through 180"; pre-transportation functions include marking, labeling, preparing shipping papers, and "Selecting a hazardous materials packaging."
- Takeaway: A 3PL that packs and ships hazmat orders performs shipper functions under federal law and must comply.

### 3.2 Training (A)
- 49 CFR 172.704, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-172/section-172.704
- Quote: "Each hazmat employee shall be provided general awareness/familiarization training"
- Also requires function-specific and safety training.
- Takeaway: Ask whether warehouse staff who pick/pack batteries or aerosols have current hazmat training and records.

### 3.3 Lithium batteries (A)
- 49 CFR 173.185, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-173/section-173.185
- Quote: "must be of the type proven to meet the criteria in part III, sub-section 38.3"
- Applies to lithium metal and lithium ion cells and batteries (definition on the section).
- Takeaway: Batteries need UN 38.3 test proof; ask the 3PL about handling and documentation. Packing, marking and state-of-charge limits are in the rest of the section (not quoted here).

### 3.4 Aerosols / limited quantities (partly verified)
- 49 CFR 173.306 title is "Limited quantities of compressed gases" and (a) sets limited-quantity package exceptions (30 kg gross weight max). I did not read text specifically naming aerosols, so do not claim aerosol specifics beyond: limited-quantity exceptions exist and shipping is mode-dependent. Confidence on aerosol claim: B/unverified.

## 4. FDA registration

### 4.1 Food facility registration (A)
- Publisher: U.S. FDA, "Questions and Answers Regarding Food Facility Registration (Seventh Edition)", guidance dated August 2018 (non-binding).
- URL: https://www.fda.gov/files/food/published/Questions-and-Answers-Regarding-Food-Facility-Registration-(Seventh-Edition).pdf
- Quote (who): "engaged in manufacturing/processing, packing, or holding of food"
- Quote (warehouse, Q B.2.3): "such a distribution center or warehouse is required to register."
- Definition (A): 21 CFR 1.227 (eCFR 2026-10-15): "Holding means storage of food", https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-1/section-1.227
- FDA program page (A): https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/registration-food-facilities-and-other-submissions , "content current as of 01/12/2026".
- Takeaway: A warehouse that stores food is a holding facility and must register unless exempt. Because holding means storage, a 3PL storing your food or supplements should be registered; ask for its FDA registration number.
- Note: FDA's third-party-logistics-specific Q&A (sole physical control language) appeared only in a search summary of a draft guidance (B). Not quoted here. Apply the 21 CFR 1.227 definition plus the Q B.2.3 warehouse answer.
- Biennial renewal, Oct 1 to Dec 31 of even years, no fee: B (search summary, not read). Verify at fda.gov before stating.

### 4.2 Cosmetics facility registration and product listing, MoCRA (A)
- Publisher: U.S. FDA, final guidance "Registration and Listing of Cosmetic Product Facilities and Products" (final guidance announced December 18, 2023).
- URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/170732/download ; news: https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-news-events/fda-issues-final-guidance-registration-and-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products
- Quote (who registers): "engages in the manufacturing or processing of a cosmetic product"
- Quote (storage exclusion, guidance text on "facility"): "solely performs one or more of the following" labeling, relabeling, packaging, repackaging, holding, distributing.
- Takeaway: A warehouse that only holds and distributes cosmetics is not a "facility" that must register. Manufacturers/processors register; the brand that is the "responsible person" lists products and needs each manufacturing facility's registration number.
- Program page (A): https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/registration-listing-cosmetic-product-facilities-and-products , "Content current as of" 09/09/2026. Quote: "A responsible person must list each marketed cosmetic product with the FDA"
- Small-business exemption: page says MoCRA exempts certain small businesses, with exceptions (e.g. injected products).
- Caveat: a 3PL that also repackages/assembles kits may be processing; the guidance defines "packaging" terms separately (not fully read). Ask the 3PL.
